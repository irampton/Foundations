# Foundations — Resources

## Resource Model

Foundations draws inspiration from CivClicker's resources. Only explicitly specified Foundations rules define behavior; missing formulas must not be inferred from an unspecified CivClicker version.

Resources are divided into Basic, Secondary, Processed, and Special resources.

## Basic Resources

### Food

Primary use:
- create Workers
- sustain population
- pay some building and technology costs

Sources:

- manual Gather Food action
- Farmers

Initial storage: **200** (one prebuilt Barn)

A Barn increases Food storage by **200**.

Granaries later double the storage contribution of Barns.

### Wood

Primary use:

- housing
- storage buildings
- workshops
- technologies
- Wonders

Sources:

- manual Cut Wood action
- Woodcutters

Initial storage: **200** (one prebuilt Wood Stockpile)

A Wood Stockpile increases Wood storage by **200**.

### Stone

Primary use:

- advanced housing
- workshops
- temples
- technologies
- Wonders

Sources:

- manual Mine Stone action
- Miners

Initial storage: **200** (one prebuilt Stone Stockpile)

A Stone Stockpile increases Stone storage by **200**.

## Secondary Resources

Secondary resources are deterministic fractional by-products of the three basic gathering jobs once the relevant discovery technology is purchased.

Discovery technologies cost basic resources. Skinning and Harvesting each cost 10 Wood; Prospecting costs 10 Stone. The [startup rules](07-rules.md#startup-and-discovery) start with no housing and use Masonry followed by a Cottage for the first Workers.

### Skins

Source:

- Farmers after **Skinning**

Uses:

- housing
- Tanneries
- Leather production
- technologies

### Herbs

Source:

- Woodcutters after **Harvesting**

Uses:

- Apothecaries
- Temples
- Graveyards
- technologies
- Cat religion

### Ore

Source:

- Miners after **Prospecting**

Uses:

- Smithies
- Metal production
- technologies

## Processed Resources

### Leather

Produced by Tanners from Skins.

Primary uses:

- Mansions
- agricultural technologies
- advanced structures
- religious costs

### Metal

Produced by Blacksmiths from Ore.

Primary uses:

- agricultural technologies
- advanced technologies
- religious costs

## Special Resources

### Science

Science is included in the first release and is produced by Librarians. Its production, job capacity, and technology costs are specified in the [implementation rules](07-rules.md#science-and-technology).

### Piety

Produced primarily by Clerics.

Piety is used for:

- unlocking Worship
- choosing a deity
- deity abilities
- advanced technologies
- some Wonder-era progression

The Cleric base production rate remains **0.05 Piety/s** before upgrades.

### Devotion

Devotion is tied to a selected deity.

- Build the deity's Altar to gain Devotion.
- Each Altar adds **+1 Devotion**.
- Deity abilities unlock at Devotion thresholds.

Devotion equals owned altar count and is never consumed. Piety costs are separate; meeting a threshold unlocks access, and priced passive abilities must then be purchased once.

### Gold

Gold exists in the original game mainly for trading and Wonder acceleration.

Because diplomacy and trade are excluded from the initial version, Gold is **reserved but inactive**.

Do not remove the data type permanently. It can be activated when Trade returns.

## Other Simulation Values

### Corpses

Corpses remain part of the health/religion system.

They are generated by worker deaths and can cause illness if not handled.

Clerics bury corpses when grave capacity is available.

Graveyards provide burial capacity.

### Sick Workers

Sickness removes Workers from productive jobs until cured.

Apothecary buildings enable Apothecary workers, who cure Sick workers.

Exact sickness and recovery rules require an explicit Foundations specification; see the [implementation rules](07-rules.md#food-health-and-happiness).

### Cats

Cats remain a special collectible/state value because they are part of the original game's identity and religion branch.

Cats can support Cat deity bonuses.

## Base Worker Production

### Farmer

Base production:

- **0.2 Food/s per Farmer** before upgrades and multipliers

Food production upgrades add directly to farmer efficiency.

Original-style bonuses retained:

- Domestication: **+0.1 Food/s per Farmer**
- Ploughshares: **+0.1 Food/s per Farmer**
- Irrigation: **+0.1 Food/s per Farmer**

Later agricultural upgrades should retain the original additive production structure.

### Woodcutter

Produces Wood automatically.

After Harvesting, Woodcutters produce 0.01 Herbs/s each before secondary-resource upgrades.

### Miner

Produces Stone automatically.

After Prospecting, Miners produce 0.01 Ore/s each before secondary-resource upgrades.

### Tanner

Converts Skins to Leather.

Maximum active Tanners are limited by Tanneries.

### Blacksmith

Converts Ore to Metal.

Maximum active Blacksmiths are limited by Smithies.

### Apothecary

Cures Sick workers.

Maximum active Apothecaries are limited by Apothecary buildings.

### Cleric

Base production:

- **0.05 Piety/s per Cleric**

Also buries corpses if Graveyard capacity exists.

### Labourer

Contributes labor toward an active Wonder.

Base Wonder work efficiency:

- **1.0 work unit/s per Labourer**

## Manual Gathering

Initial manual click yield:

- Food: 1
- Wood: 1
- Stone: 1

Later technologies improve click output while preserving the original incremental-game role of clicking.

## Storage Behavior

Basic Resources are capped by storage.

When storage is full:

- automatic production for that resource is wasted;
- manual gathering cannot push the resource above capacity;
- secondary-resource production continues independently even at full basic storage.

Only Food, Wood, and Stone have storage caps. Secondary resources, processed resources, Science, and Piety are uncapped.

## Exact simulation rules

The [implementation rules](07-rules.md#production-and-storage) define every job rate, conversion, modifier, and fractional calculation. [Food, health, and Happiness](07-rules.md#food-health-and-happiness) defines upkeep, starvation, sickness, burial, healing, and Happiness. Start with 200 Food, 200 Wood, and 200 Stone, exactly one corresponding storage building each, and no Workers or housing.
